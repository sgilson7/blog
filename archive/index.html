  • 7 October 2026 — Why people walk looking at their phones Walking to a lecture hall an hour early, reading email I could have read sitting down, I started wondering why a freed-up attention gets filled that way. ICAP tells me scrolling is the engagement least likely to teach me anything, and says nothing about why it is the easiest to start. My working answer is context: a familiar walk builds an expectation, so a small departure from it is worth noticing, and an app replaces the context faster than one can form. This is a line of thinking that needs a literature review, including work that would contradict it.

  • 2 October 2026 — Thinking about curriculum as a knowledge graph I want to explore how a curriculum might help students build connections between knowledge components, and how working through concrete problems might make those connections easier to draw. Starting with cats and multisets, I work through a graph account of remembering and an experience connecting interpolation to recurrence relations. These are provisional ideas that need a literature review, including work that could contradict them, before I use them to design a combinatorics unit.
